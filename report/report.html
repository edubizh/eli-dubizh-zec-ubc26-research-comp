The variant perception

Consensus treats "fast and cheap" as Solana's pitch — but that is commoditized; every new L1 and L2 is fast and cheap. Our claim is non-obvious: in a world of abundant blockspace, the scarce, value-accruing resource is execution quality — uptime, deterministic sub-second finality, low landed-transaction failure, predictable fees. The market already pays a large, explicit premium for it: Hyperliquid (HYPE) — a Long >$250M finalist in this competition last cycle — trades at ~27× protocol revenue precisely because it is fast, never down, and routes ~99% of fees to buybacks. On October 21, 2026, Solana swapped its entire consensus engine — the 18-month Alpenglow upgrade — in a single slot with zero downtime (a first for any live chain), cutting finality from ~12.8s to a ~150ms target under a hardened "20+20" fault model. That converts its biggest historical liability — seven outages through early 2024 — into a measurable strength: ~30 months of 100% uptime. Alpenglow lets a $53B base layer earn the execution re-rating a single app already enjoys, at a scale no app can match.

Why the fundamentals fit — and the objection we answer first

Solana is #1 in application revenue for nine straight quarters (~$299M in Q3 2026, +48% QoQ, ~$1.2B annualized — Blockworks), #1 in DEX volume, and sits on an all-time-high ~$16.8B stablecoin base; network REV just inflected +48% QoQ, its first rise in six quarters. We will not pretend SOL is statistically cheap — on raw network REV it trades near 175×. But that denominator is broken for every base layer in abundant blockspace (Ethereum screens above 1,000×); the right lenses — app revenue (~44×, re-accelerating) and the execution re-rating — point the other way. You are buying a mispriced factor at a 69% drawdown, not a cheap token.

Project Overview
Solana (SOL) · Long · Over $250M
1

What Solana is now: from "fast chain" to settlement layer

Solana is a single-state, high-throughput Layer-1 that settles transactions in one global execution environment rather than fragmenting liquidity across rollups. For most of its life the bull case was performance and the bear case was reliability. In 2026 that framing is obsolete. Throughput is no longer scarce — it is a race to zero across dozens of chains. What is scarce is a venue that institutions and high-value applications can trust to be always on, fast to finality, and cheap to verify. Delphi Digital frames Solana's 2026 upgrade cycle as building the "decentralized Nasdaq" — an exchange-grade environment where an onchain central-limit order book can compete with a centralized exchange on latency and fairness. That is the lens that matters, and it is the lens this panel's institutions — a tokenized-fund issuer (Franklin Templeton), a financial-infrastructure fund (Collab+Currency), and the research shop that invented the REV metric (Blockworks) — already use.

2

The engineering turn: Alpenglow (the core of this thesis)

Alpenglow is the largest change to Solana since launch, and the detail matters because the thesis is an engineering one. It replaces TowerBFT with two components. Votor is a new voting and finality path using BLS signature aggregation and a two-round fast/slow design: a block finalizes in one round at ≥80% stake participation and two rounds at ≥60%. Votes move off-chain, which both collapses finality (from ~12.8s to a ~150ms target) and frees an estimated 70%+ of block space previously consumed by vote transactions. Rotor (the propagation layer that succeeds Turbine) is the next stage and is not yet live — we treat it as upside, not as a claim. The economic redesign is underappreciated: vote fees are replaced by a burned ~0.8 SOL/epoch "validator admission ticket," cutting validator operating costs by roughly 60% and making the validator set cheaper to join and more sustainable to run.

The resilience upgrade is the part institutions will care about: Alpenglow's "20+20" model keeps finalizing through 20% actively malicious stake and 20% crashed/offline stake at the same time, a materially stronger guarantee than TowerBFT's <33% single-fault threshold. And the migration itself is the proof point — Anza executed an 18-month build as a one-slot "hot swap" on live mainnet with zero downtime, the first time any network has replaced its consensus engine without halting. It was rehearsed across nine at-scale migrations and 1,500-node simulations and shipped with a reversible controlled-pause window. This is not a roadmap promise a judge has to take on faith; it is a dated, verifiable engineering milestone.

The weakness-to-strength pivot (the whole argument in four numbers)

7 full-network halts between Dec 2020 and Feb 2024  ·  Feb 6, 2024 last outage (~5 hours)  ·  ~30 months of 100% uptime since  ·  ~12.8s → ~150ms finality via a zero-downtime migration. The market still prices the first number. We are underwriting the last three.

3

Market analysis & traction: the economic engine

Strip out the narrative and Solana is, by the metrics this competition is judged on, the most economically active base layer in crypto. It has led all chains in application revenue for nine consecutive quarters, printed ~$299M in Q3 2026 (+48% QoQ), and carries the deepest onchain stablecoin base outside Ethereum and Tron at an all-time-high ~$16.8B. Its network REV — Blockworks' fees-plus-MEV metric — collapsed ~87% as the 2024–25 memecoin cycle unwound, and then inflected +48% QoQ in Q3 2026, the first increase in six quarters. That inflection, off a washed-out base, is the fundamental tell that the franchise is broadening beyond speculation into payments, DeFi, and tokenized assets.

The revenue is diversified across real applications, not one casino: Jupiter (the dominant swap router), Raydium and the broader DEX complex (Solana has out-traded Ethereum in monthly DEX volume since late 2024), Pump.fun on the consumer side, plus fast-growing stablecoin payments and tokenized-equity volume. Daily active addresses average ~2.3M. The point is not that any single app is irreplaceable; it is that demand is broad, which is exactly what makes a base-layer bet more durable than a single-app bet like HYPE.

4

Token assessment & value accrual

SOL has ~590M circulating of ~636M total supply. Issuance is ~3.6% and disinflating 15%/yr toward a 1.5% floor; ~68–69% of supply is staked (~$38B of economic security), earning a ~5–6% gross yield. Value accrues to the token three ways: (i) base + priority fees and MEV paid to stakers (REV), (ii) a protocol burn on fees, and (iii) the staking yield itself, now net-positive in real terms for most validators post-Alpenglow cost cuts.

Demand is structurally new. Spot ETFs are live and staking-enabled — Franklin's SOEZ (0.19%) and Bitwise's BSOL among them — holding ~$1.3B+ and growing, with more issuers filed. Digital-asset treasury companies have accumulated tens of millions of SOL. Against ~3.6% issuance, ETF + staking + treasury lockup is removing float faster than the chain is minting it — a supply/demand setup the memecoin-era price does not reflect.

Valuation — three lenses, one conclusion

(1) Reframe the multiple. We concede SOL is not cheap on network REV (~175×). But that metric has broken down for every base layer as blockspace became abundant — Ethereum screens above 1,000× on the same denominator. The economically meaningful multiple is price-to-app revenue, where Solana trades near 44× on ~$1.2B annualized and is growing (+48% QoQ), versus a shrinking base elsewhere. (2) Price the factor. The market pays ~27× protocol revenue for Hyperliquid's execution quality; Alpenglow qualifies Solana for the same factor at base-layer scale and diversification. (3) Supply/demand. Staking-enabled ETFs, DAT accumulation, and ~68% staked lockup are removing float faster than ~3.6% issuance adds it.

Conclusion
Solana (SOL) · Long · Over $250M

Solana spent four years being underwritten on the thing it did worst — staying online. On October 21, 2026 it retired that liability in a single slot, and in doing so it quietly changed what kind of asset it is: from a high-beta throughput bet into the only $50B-scale base layer that can credibly claim exchange-grade reliability and sub-second finality. The market has not repriced that, because it is still watching memecoin REV and ETF flows — both near cyclical lows — instead of the variable that actually drives re-ratings.

We know the market pays for execution quality because it already does: Hyperliquid earns ~27× revenue for being fast and never down. Alpenglow makes the same case for Solana, but on a franchise with nine straight quarters of #1 app revenue, an all-time-high stablecoin base, a freshly inflecting REV line, and structural ETF and staking demand shrinking its float. Buying at ~$89 — 69% below the prior high, three weeks after the catalyst — is buying a mispriced factor with the downside already discounted.

Recommendation: Long SOL, 12–18 months, base target $160 (+80%), bull $290 (+226%), bear $55 (−38%). The reliability is proven. The re-rating is not yet priced. That gap is the trade.
